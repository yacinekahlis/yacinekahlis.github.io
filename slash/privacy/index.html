Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 13, 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how Slash (“we”, “us”) collects, uses, and protects your information when you use the Slash mobile application (the “App”), published by Yacine Kahlis. By using the App you agree to the practices described here.

The short version: Slash has no account, no login, and never asks for your name or email. Your rolls (the photos you picked and the shots made from them) and your settings are stored on your iPhone. When you shoot, the photo you picked is sent over an encrypted connection to our server and to our AI provider, whose image model makes the shot. Our server deletes your photo as soon as the shoot finishes, and keeps the shot for 72 hours so your iPhone can download it. We do not use your photos to train anything. No ads, we never sell your data, and we do not track you across other apps.

Your anonymous identifier

The App has no accounts. The first time you open it, it creates a random, anonymous identifier on your iPhone. It is not tied to your name, email or Apple Account. The App sends it with its requests to our server, which uses it to check your plan, count your trial and weekly shots, and apply fair-use limits. The same identifier is used by RevenueCat, which manages subscriptions for us, and by our analytics provider, both described below.

The identifier is kept in your iPhone's Keychain: it stays the same if you delete the App and install it again, and it can move to a new iPhone with an encrypted backup. “Delete all my data” in the App's Settings replaces it with a new one.

Information stored on your iPhone only

  • Your rolls: the photos you picked to reshoot and the shots made from them, with their tags.
  • Reels: the before-and-after videos the App made for you, kept in its temporary files so you can save or share them.
  • Settings: whether to save the original photo too, whether your reels carry the Slash mark, and your trial reminder.
  • Onboarding choices: the looks you picked, used to set up the App (they are also sent to analytics, see below).
  • App state: your plan as the App last saw it, and simple counters used to choose when to ask for an App Store review.
  • Public data: the catalogue of tags and looks the App offers. It says nothing about you.

This data lives in the App's local storage on your iPhone. It may be included in the device backups you configure with Apple (iCloud or local backups). Those backups are managed by Apple under your Apple Account; we have no access to them.

Photos sent to be reshot

You choose the photo with iOS's own photo picker, which gives the App only the photo you pick. When you shoot, the App sends that one photo, resized and saved as a JPEG, and the tags you chose over an encrypted connection to our server. Our server passes them to our AI provider, whose image model makes the shot. Our server deletes your photo as soon as that shoot finishes.

The shot itself stays on our server for 72 hours, so that your iPhone can download it, also when the App was closed in the meantime. After that it is deleted, by a clean-up that runs every hour. “Delete all my data” deletes it at once.

Our AI provider handles your photo under its own terms for business (API) customers; how long it keeps a request is set by those terms, not by us. It may refuse a photo or a request under its own content rules; that shot is then not counted. We do not use your photos to train anything.

Reels

To make a reel, the short before-and-after video of a shot, the App sends the original photo, the shot and its tags to our server, which renders the video and returns it. Both pictures and the video are deleted from our server before it answers. Nothing is kept.

Saving and sharing

A shot or a reel goes to your photo library only when you save it, and to another app (Messages, Instagram, TikTok or any app in the iOS share sheet) only when you share it. What you share is then handled by that app under its own terms.

Information kept on our server

  • Shoots waiting for your iPhone: each shoot is kept with your anonymous identifier, its tags, its status and, once it is made, its image, for 72 hours (see above). The photo you sent is removed from it as soon as the shoot finishes.
  • Usage records: for each shot our AI provider is asked for, we record your anonymous identifier, the time, what the shot was counted on (the onboarding shot, the free trial or the subscription), the AI model and the quality used, how long it took, what it cost us and the outcome (made, refused, failed). Never the photo, and nothing about what it shows. These records enforce fair-use limits, control our costs and help prevent abuse; they are kept for 45 days, then deleted automatically.
  • Your onboarding shot: a record, linked to your anonymous identifier, that your iPhone used its one free onboarding shot. It is kept until you delete your data (see below).
  • Weekly shot counter: the number of shots you used in each week of your subscription, linked to your anonymous identifier. A week's counter is deleted 35 days after that week began.
  • Trial shot counter: the number of shots used during a free trial. It is linked to the App Store purchase of the trial (the product, the original purchase date and, when the store gives it, the transaction number), not to your identifier, so the trial's shots stay with the purchase: reinstalling the App, restoring it on another iPhone or deleting your data does not reset them. It is deleted 30 days after the trial ends. In the rare case the store gives no purchase date, the counter is linked to your identifier instead, and Delete all my data erases it.
  • Short-lived memory: your plan as RevenueCat last reported it (24 hours at most) and your request counts for the current minute.
  • Technical logs: our server's logs record events such as a shoot's tags, its outcome and how long it took. They never contain a photo, and they do not contain your identifier itself, only a pseudonym computed from it. They are kept by our hosting provider for a limited time under its own retention rules.

Our server sees the IP address your requests come from, as any server does. It uses it only to limit the number of requests one address can make and does not store it. Our hosting provider's network may record it in its own logs for a limited time.

Purchases

Subscriptions are bought and paid through Apple; we never see your name, email or payment details. RevenueCat manages subscriptions for us: it receives your anonymous identifier, your purchase and subscription status from the App Store, and how many times the App showed you its subscription screen. Our server asks RevenueCat whether your identifier has an active plan, and RevenueCat notifies our server when a subscription changes.

Analytics

We use an analytics provider, whose servers are in the United States, to understand how the App is used. It receives product-usage events tied to your anonymous identifier: the screens you view and the actions you take (for example a shoot started, finished or failed and how long it took, the tags used, a shot saved or shared, a reel made, the subscription screen viewed, a purchase or a restore), your onboarding choices, your plan, and the technical details its library adds (device model, iOS version, App version and language). It may use the IP address of a request to estimate an approximate location. It never receives your photos, your shots or anything about what they show. There are no session recordings.

The same analytics project is shared by the apps of the same developer; each event says which app it comes from.

Permissions

  • Choosing a photo: the App uses iOS's own photo picker, which needs no permission and gives the App only the photo you pick. The App never reads your photo library.
  • Photos (add only): asked the first time you save a shot or a reel. It lets the App add to your photo library, not read it.
  • Notifications: local only: a reminder the day before your free trial ends, if you turn it on, and “Your shot is ready” when a shot finishes while you are outside the App. They are scheduled on your iPhone and not sent through a server; no notification token is collected.

Third-party services

  • Apple: processes App Store payments. Apple may also collect standard diagnostics (such as crash reports) according to your device settings and Apple's privacy policy.
  • An AI provider: makes your shots from the photos and tags you send, as described above.
  • RevenueCat: manages subscriptions, as described above. See RevenueCat's privacy policy.
  • An analytics provider: receives the usage events described above.
  • An app update service: delivers App updates. When the App checks for an update, its servers receive standard technical information about the request, such as the App version.
  • A cloud hosting provider: runs our server and its database, and keeps our server's logs.

We do not show ads, we do not sell your personal information or share it for cross-context behavioral advertising, and we do not track you across other companies' apps or websites.

Where your data is processed

Our service providers, including our AI provider, RevenueCat and our analytics provider, process data in the United States and possibly in other countries. Your information may therefore be processed outside Quebec and outside Canada, under the laws of those places.

Deleting your data

In the App, open Settings and tap Delete all my data. It needs an internet connection: our server is asked first, and if it cannot be reached, nothing is deleted.

  • On our server, it deletes your shoots with their images (and the photo of a shoot still running), the record of your onboarding shot, your weekly shot counters, and a trial shot counter linked to your identifier if there is one, and anonymises your usage records: your identifier is replaced by a random code, so they can no longer be linked to you. They still count toward our overall daily limits and are deleted after 45 days like all usage records. The server also forgets the copy of your plan it holds in memory. The per-minute request counts expire on their own within a minute.
  • On your iPhone, it erases your rolls (your photos and your shots), your reels, your settings, your onboarding choices, the App's state and counters, its temporary files and cached pictures, and turns off your trial reminder. The App then gets a new anonymous identifier and starts over as on its first launch. The public catalogue of tags and looks is kept.
  • What it does not erase: the shots and reels you already saved to your photo library or shared; your subscription, which stays with Apple (cancel it in your Apple Account, see below); your purchase history at Apple and RevenueCat; the trial shot counter linked to your App Store purchase, deleted 30 days after the trial ends; what our AI provider may still hold of a request under its own terms; analytics events already sent to our analytics provider, which stay under your old identifier (the App does not link them to the new one); and technical logs, which expire under our hosting provider's retention rules. To get your subscription back afterwards, tap Restore.

Deleting the App removes your rolls, your reels and your settings from your iPhone, but not your anonymous identifier, which stays in the Keychain, and not the records on our server: use Delete all my data first if you want those erased too. Cancelling a subscription is done in your Apple Account (Settings → your name → Subscriptions); neither deleting the App nor deleting your data cancels it.

You can also ask us by email to delete the records tied to your anonymous identifier on our server, at RevenueCat or at our analytics provider. Because the App has no accounts, we will reply with what we need to find your data.
